About 
------
Liam studied an integrated masters in physics at University College London, with a year abroad at the University of Washington. During this time he worked on Na-ion anode materials with the [Clean Materials Technology Group](https://jawwaddarr.wixsite.com/my-site), van der Waals superconductors with [Professor Chris Howard](https://profiles.ucl.ac.uk/1427-chris-howard), and computational study of laser ablation of silicon with [Professor Dorothy Duffy](https://profiles.ucl.ac.uk/6921-dorothy-duffy/about). He subsequently obtained his PhD in physics in the [Dutton Group](duttongroup.weebly.com) at the University of Cambridge, working at the interface between materials physics and solid state chemistry to study Na-ion battery cathode materials, before transitioning to a postdoc in the [Strange Group](https://web.slac.stanford.edu/strangegroup) at the [Stanford Synchrotron Radiation Lightsource (SSRL)](https://www-ssrl.slac.stanford.edu/) of Stanford University, working on materials characterisation for water-splitting applications as part of the [H2NEW consortium](https://h2new.energy.gov/). 

Liam is now at the Department of Materials of the University of Oxford as a postdoc in the [Energy Materials Interfaces Group](https://emi.materials.ox.ac.uk/), using synchrotron techniques to study degradation of battery cathodes in affilation with the [Faraday Institution's LEAP project](https://degradationproject.com/).
